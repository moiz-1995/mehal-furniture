# Build Mehal's configurator on free, open foundations

Mehal Furniture can build almost all of its website and design app on free, commercially licensed tools. The real costs are three: about **$400–1,500 once** for custom 3D models of the bestseller pieces, about **$0.02–0.05 per AI image edit**, and about **$20 a month** for hosting that is allowed to run a business. The recommended stack is a Next.js site with **React Three Fiber + drei** for the 3D configurator, Google's **`<model-viewer>`** for the "see it in your room" AR button, **Poly Haven and ambientCG (both CC0)** for fabric, wood and HDRI lighting, **Nano Banana 2.1 ($0.034 per 1K image, $0.017 in batch)** as the default AI redesign model, **GSAP (now fully free, plugins included) + Lenis + Motion** for animation, **Cormorant + Manrope** (OFL) with **Gulzar or Noto Nastaliq Urdu** for type, and **Supabase** plus a simple phone-friendly admin page for the owner. The market case is strong. The research found **no Pakistani furniture site with a 3D configurator**, while local made-to-order rivals already take 40–60% advances and sell through WhatsApp, so a visual "design your own piece" tool that ends in a WhatsApp message would be new in Rawalpindi. The main risk is not technology or cost. It is customers expecting exactly what an AI image showed. Every design option should therefore map to something the workshop can actually build, and every AI image should be labelled as a concept.

## No Pakistani rival lets customers design on screen

The large chains run conventional e-commerce. **Interwood** shows PKR prices, a cart, a WhatsApp chat button, AR viewing of 3D models and a "Wedding Package Builder", and its corner sofas can be customised in "materials and colors of your choice" through consultation, not on screen ([interwood.pk](https://interwood.pk/); [Interwood sofas](https://interwood.pk/collections/sofas?page=2)). **Habitt** puts WhatsApp order support on every page, offers instalments and interior design services, and has no AR ([habitt.com](https://habitt.com/)); one directory lists it as "Custom Furniture: No" ([nmfurnisher](https://nmfurnisher.com/best-furniture-shops-in-pakistan/)). **No Pakistani furniture site with a confirmed 3D configurator was found.** The tools that exist are abroad, such as Sackit's ([Sackit](https://sackit.eu/pages/3d-configurator)). A Pakistani maker's own review of the market says many brands "focus primarily on standardized production, which means limited flexibility in sizing, fabric choices, or wood customization" ([solidwood.pk](https://solidwood.pk/best-furniture-brands-in-pakistan/)).

Mehal's nearest competitors are made-to-order workshops in Islamabad and Rawalpindi. **Renome** (I-11) makes every order custom and takes a **60% advance**. **Poshish** quotes 20–25 working days and a 60% deposit. **ROCO** (Multan) asks 40% and takes WhatsApp orders. Habitt asks 50–100% with 15–25 working days' lead time. These figures come from a guide written by ROCO, a competitor, so treat them as indicative ([ROCO guide](https://roco.pk/guide/best-furniture-brands-in-pakistan/)). Sheesham (tali) and deodar (diyar) are the main solid woods, with MDF/lasani for budget work ([Avenir](https://avenirdevelopments.com/house-construction-cost/woodwork-and-carpentry-costs-in-pakistan)). Chiniot's carvers, who rely on sheesham and walnut, meet most national demand ([TDAP report](https://tdap.gov.pk/wp-content/uploads/2023/10/Report-on-Wooden-Furniture-Updated-on-17-Oct-2023.pdf); [Tribune](https://tribune.com.pk/story/2055213/inflation-eating-chiniots-exquisite-furniture)). Carved sets are still bought as dowry ([Dawn](https://www.dawn.com/news/1128848/wood-you-believe-it)), which makes a bridal or jahez bundle of bedroom, dining and sofa a natural package for Mehal's carved dining sets.

Buying habits decide the shape of the app. A nationally representative Gallup & Gilani survey found that **6 in 10 online shoppers prefer cash on delivery** ([Dawn](https://www.dawn.com/news/1962597)). WhatsApp Pay is unavailable in Pakistan, so social sellers share Easypaisa, JazzCash and bank details by hand in chat ([DHL](https://www.dhl.com/discover/en-pk/e-commerce-advice/e-commerce-trends/social-commerce-strategy-pakistan)). Discovery happens on Facebook (about **69 million** users) and Instagram (about **26.6 million**) ([NapoleonCat](https://stats.napoleoncat.com/social-media-users-in-pakistan/2026/)). The app's main call to action should therefore be **"Send this design on WhatsApp"**, not "Checkout". It should show a "from Rs X" price range for custom work, which competitors do not do, and state the advance percentage, the lead time and the wood and foam warranty up front. Online checkout can come later through **Safepay**, which quotes 2.5% including tax with no fixed fees ([Safepay help](https://safepay.helpscoutdocs.com/article/120-how-much-does-safepay-charge)), though a third-party profile lists 2.9% + Rs 30 ([extruct.ai](https://www.extruct.ai/hub/getsafepay-pk/)), so get a written quote. The State Bank has also told online stores to support Raast ([ProPakistani](https://propakistani.pk/2024/11/29/sbp-orders-to-enable-raast-payments-in-online-shopping-stores/)).

## Free CC0 assets cover materials; hero pieces need about $400–1,500 of custom modelling

**Textures and lighting are solved for free.** Everything on Poly Haven (models, textures, HDRIs) is **CC0**: commercial use is allowed, no credit is needed, and the files can ship inside a paid product ([Poly Haven licence](https://polyhaven.com/license)). Its fabric category holds **43 textures**, including `velour_velvet`, `wool_boucle`, `rough_linen`, six leathers, `quatrefoil_jacquard_fabric` (a burgundy damask brocade) and `floral_jacquard` ([Poly Haven fabric API](https://api.polyhaven.com/assets?type=textures&categories=fabric)). ambientCG is also **CC0 1.0** and adds fabric, leather, wood, painted wood, marble and metal categories ([ambientCG licence](https://docs.ambientcg.com/license/); [ambientCG fabrics](https://ambientcg.com/list?type=material&q=fabric)). The practical approach is about six base weaves (velvet, linen, boucle, leather, chenille, jacquard) stored with a neutral base colour and **tinted in code**, so 30 colours do not need 30 texture sets. Velvet should use the glTF sheen extension for its soft highlight. Gold legs need no texture at all: a metal material with low roughness and a Poly Haven HDRI for reflections is enough. No CC0 sheesham texture was found, so the best option is to photograph Mehal's own wood and fabric stock. Those photos are free, and they show materials the workshop can actually buy.

**Free 3D models are useful for prototypes but not for the catalogue.** Poly Haven's furniture category has **85 CC0 models**, including five sofas, three armchairs, eight coffee tables, a dining table and chairs, an ottoman, `ClassicConsole_01`, `gothic_coffee_table` and `ornate_mirror_01` ([Poly Haven furniture API](https://api.polyhaven.com/assets?type=models&categories=furniture)). Kenney's Furniture Kit (about 140 files) and Quaternius's pack are CC0 but low-poly and cartoon-like ([Kenney](https://kenney.nl/assets/furniture-kit); [OpenGameArt](https://opengameart.org/content/furniture-low-poly)). They are fine for wireframes and wrong for a luxury brand. The sofas that came up on Sketchfab are **CC-BY**, so the creator must be credited ([Sketchfab example](https://sketchfab.com/3d-models/sofa-dfec02dbf6b44839aed663693e3f170a)). A Chesterfield there is the closest match to a tufted sofa, but its licence was not checked ([Sketchfab Chesterfield](https://sketchfab.com/3d-models/vintage-chesterfield-sofa-3d-model-game-ready-25c5b33b6cf343c89d8c46bc2acb9c6c)). The best test asset is Khronos's **SheenChair**, made by Wayfair and released as **CC0**. It already contains fabric material variants and sheen, so it can prove the whole pipeline (variant switching, compression, AR) before any custom model exists ([Khronos glTF Sample Assets](https://github.com/KhronosGroup/glTF-Sample-Assets/blob/main/Models/Models-showcase.md)). None of the free models is split into separate arms, legs and seats, and none matches Mehal's tufted cream sofas with gold legs or its carved dining sets.

**Custom models are therefore the one real asset cost.** Fiverr sofa-modelling gigs are priced in bands of **up to $50, $50–100 and $100–200**, with some Blender furniture sellers starting at **$15** ([Fiverr sofa models](https://www.fiverr.com/morefx/create-3d-sofa-models); [Fiverr Blender furniture](https://fiverr.com/bobe32/3d-model-furniture-in-blender)). One Freelancer bidder quoted **$15 / $30 / $40** per model with GLB web optimisation included ([Freelancer](https://www.freelancer.com.au/projects/blender/photorealistic-furniture-modeling)), and modular-sofa configurator gigs exist ([Fiverr modular sofa GLB](https://fiverr.com/higq_animation/furniture-product-design-modular-sofa-model-configurator)). A configurable sofa is several models in one: three or four arm styles, three or four leg types, two backs and a repeatable seat module, all modelled to real dimensions. That puts it at the top of these bands, so **four to six hero pieces should cost roughly $400–1,500**. This is an estimate based on the price bands, and a local Rawalpindi or Islamabad Blender artist could be cheaper. Brief the modeller with real dimensions in cm (1 unit = 1 m), named parts, the origin at floor centre, PBR materials, under about 50k triangles per piece, a separate tufting normal map, and delivery of both the GLB and the .blend source file.

**AI image-to-3D is for placeholders and decor, not hero products.** The **TRELLIS.2** model from Microsoft is **MIT-licensed** and costs about **$0.02 per generation** on fal.ai. **Hunyuan3D**'s community licence excludes only the EU, UK and South Korea, so it can be used from Pakistan. Meshy and Tripo cost about $0.10–0.30 per model through their APIs. All of these figures come from a vendor's June 2026 survey ([3D AI Studio](https://www.3daistudio.com/state-of-ai-3d-generation-2026)). Meshy's own help page says **free-tier outputs are CC BY 4.0** and need credit, while paid users own their models ([Meshy help](https://help.meshy.ai/en/articles/9992001-who-owns-the-models-generated-by-meshy)). These tools produce a single fused mesh with baked textures, so arms cannot be swapped and fabric cannot be changed cleanly, and fine tufting and gold carving come out blurred. Use them for vases, lamps and rugs in room scenes, or as a rough draft for a Blender artist to rebuild.

## Swap parts and repeat seats in React Three Fiber; add model-viewer only for AR

The approach to shape changes matters more than the choice of library. Proven configurators **split the model into parts, scale or repeat only the middle section, and move the end pieces outward**, so edges and texture coordinates never stretch ([three.js forum, door configurator](https://discourse.threejs.org/t/scaling-and-positioning-in-door-configurator/52902)). Wawa Sensei's open-source React Three Fiber table configurator does exactly this. It scales only the tabletop on x, moves the legs to the new ends, and swaps leg styles by showing one of three leg meshes from the same glTF file ([tutorial](https://dev.to/wawasensei/creating-a-3d-table-configurator-with-react-three-fiber-1dl2); [repo](https://github.com/wass08/table-configurator-three-js-r3F-tutorial-final)). Morph targets are the tempting shortcut, but developers report that they **stretch textures** in ways triplanar mapping does not fix, and that the final size is hard to predict, which is a problem when a customer orders an "84-inch" sofa ([three.js forum](https://discourse.threejs.org/t/cannot-get-rid-of-texture-stretching-with-morphtarget/67598); [size prediction thread](https://discourse.threejs.org/t/how-to-predict-the-size-of-a-mesh-with-morph-targets/23567)).

For Mehal, this means **one Blender master file per base piece**, exported as one GLB with named parts: left and right arms in rolled, track and shelter styles; gold tapered, turned wood and block legs; low, high and tufted backs; and a single `Seat_Module` repeated two, three or four times, plus corner and chaise modules for L-shapes. Repeating whole seat modules suits made-to-order sizes and keeps the tufting buttons lined up correctly. Morphs are acceptable only for small changes such as back height or cushion fullness. Tables follow the Wawa pattern directly: scale the top and move the legs, with carved edges kept as a separate mesh. The same configuration record (base, arms, legs, back, seats, L-shape, fabric, colour) should drive the 3D view, the price range, the WhatsApp summary and the workshop cutting list.

**React Three Fiber + drei** is the right library because the arm, leg and L-shape logic needs full control inside a React/Next.js app. Load the canvas client-side only. **Babylon.js** can do the job but fits React less naturally, and its ecosystem for this use is smaller. **`<model-viewer>`** is the fastest path for pieces where only the material changes (a bed, a coffee table). It supports **KHR_materials_variants**, so one GLB can carry several finishes, and a scene-graph API changes colour, metalness, roughness and textures ([model-viewer scenegraph](https://modelviewer.dev/examples/scenegraph/)). Its real value for Mehal is AR. It opens **WebXR or Scene Viewer on Android (Chrome 83+, with ARCore floor detection)** and **Quick Look on iOS**, generating the iOS USDZ file **automatically from the GLB** when no `ios-src` is given. With **`ar-scale="fixed"`** the customer cannot resize the piece, so they see the true size of the sofa in their own lounge ([model-viewer AR](https://modelviewer.dev/examples/augmentedreality/)). There is one limitation: material edits made by page scripts appear in AR only in WebXR mode, because Scene Viewer and Quick Look are separate native apps. For a configured sofa, export the current scene to a GLB, upload it to storage, and pass that HTTPS URL to model-viewer. Many budget Infinix, Tecno and Samsung A-series phones may not support ARCore. This was not checked, so hide the AR button when model-viewer reports that AR is unavailable and show a "measure your space" guide instead.

Mid-range Android phones set the performance budget. The `gltf-transform` command-line tool compresses meshes with Draco or Meshopt and textures with KTX2/Basis or WebP in one `optimize` pass ([gltf-transform CLI](https://gltf-transform.dev/cli)). Community guides report that **KTX2 textures use 4–8× less GPU memory** than JPEG or PNG, and that Meshopt decodes faster than Draco on mobile ([glTF compression guide](https://jvinhit.github.io/blog/threejs-16-gltf-compression-draco-meshopt-ktx2/)). Reasonable targets, which are engineering estimates and not benchmarks, are: each GLB under 3–5 MB, under 150k visible triangles, 1–2K textures, screen resolution (DPR) capped at 1.5, `ContactShadows` instead of real-time shadows, fabric textures loaded only when their swatch is tapped, and a still render shown first while the 3D viewer loads. For the interface, copy the step order that **Burrow** and **Flexsteel** use: base and size first, then arms, legs, fabric and colour ([Burrow](https://burrow.com/range-fabric/sleeper); [Flexsteel Moxy](https://www.flexsteel.com/collections/moxy)). Use picture thumbnails for each shape option, a sticky price-range bar, measurements in inches and cm on the model, and a shareable URL for each design.

## AI redesign costs cents; guardrails matter more than the model

For pieces without a 3D model, and for photos customers upload as inspiration, instruction-based image editing is now cheap and good. On the arena.ai image-edit leaderboard (16 Nov 2026), **OpenAI's GPT-Image-2.5/2.6** family holds the top spots, followed by **MAI-Image-2.7** and **Grok Imagine**. **Gemini Nano Banana 2.1** ranks **#9**, and **Seedream 5.0 Pro** ranks #14 ([arena.ai](https://arena.ai/leaderboard/image-edit)). **No first-party image API has a free tier**, so the only "free" routes are aggregator signup credits or self-hosted open models, and a GPU server costs more than API calls at Mehal's volume.

Both the Gemini API and OpenAI's API list Pakistan as a supported country ([Google regions](https://ai.google.dev/gemini-api/docs/available-regions.md.txt); [OpenAI help](https://help.openai.com/articles/5347006)). Call providers directly rather than through aggregators: fal charges **$0.08** for a Nano Banana 2 edit that costs **$0.067** direct ([fal pricing](https://fal.ai/pricing)). Use fal only for open models and for segmentation. Note that the original `gemini-2.5-flash-image` shuts down on **15 March 2027**, so do not build on it ([Google pricing](https://aistudio.google.com/docs/pricing)). At $0.03–0.05 per edit, **1,000 customer edits cost about $30–50 a month**.

Every major model family now accepts several input images. That allows the key request: "apply the fabric in image 2 to the sofa in image 1, keep the shape, legs, background and camera angle". gpt-image's documentation shows four inputs ([OpenAI image guide](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/guides/image-generation)), and FLUX 3 charges nothing extra for references ([bfl.ai](https://bfl.ai/pricing)). Store photos of the workshop's real fabric stock as the reference swatches, so the model copies a fabric Mehal can actually source rather than inventing one from text. To stop the background and the rest of the room changing, create a furniture mask with **SAM 3 ($0.005 on fal)** ([fal pricing](https://fal.ai/pricing)), run the edit, then blend only the masked area back onto the original photo. This step matters because OpenAI's own masks are "prompt-based" and the model "may not follow its exact shape with complete precision" ([OpenAI image guide](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/guides/image-generation)). Always regenerate from the master photo using the chosen options, never from the previous output, and cache results by (photo, options, model) so repeated choices cost nothing.

For uploaded inspiration photos, the strongest flow turns the photo into an order specification. A vision model extracts the furniture type, arm style, legs, fabric, colour and pattern as structured data. An image-embedding search then finds the nearest Mehal pieces. The app shows "closest we make" alongside "customise to match", using Mehal's real option lists. Wayfair's Decorify linked its AI output to its own catalogue in the same way ([Retail Dive](https://www.retaildive.com/news/wayfair-decorify-home-design-product-visualization-tool/689001/)). The accuracy of this flow on furniture has not been measured, so test it on Mehal's own catalogue before launch.

The industry evidence on risk is consistent. One furniture executive says AI "just cannot iterate to get something that meets our quality standards", and at Samuel & Sons clients asked how to sew a trim that existed only in an edited image: "It was physically impossible". Some retailers ban AI imagery outright because inaccurate pictures erode trust and increase returns ([Business of Home, Oct 2026](https://businessofhome.com/articles/how-ai-generated-imagery-is-quietly-reshaping-the-furniture-business)). Mehal's defences follow from this. Customers choose from constrained pickers (fabric SKU, leg SKU, size template), with free text as an optional extra. Each output carries a "Concept preview, final piece may differ" badge. The specification sheet, not the picture, is the contract, and a craftsman confirms the design on WhatsApp before quoting. To control cost and abuse, require a phone login, set about 10 edits per user per day, cap total monthly spend, run a moderation check on uploads, strip location data from photos, and use the batch API at half price to pre-render each catalogue sofa in its top fabrics.

## GSAP is now free, and restraint reads as luxury

**GSAP is 100% free for commercial use, including every former Club plugin** (SplitText, ScrollSmoother, MorphSVG, DrawSVG, Flip, Inertia), thanks to Webflow's backing ([GSAP pricing](https://gsap.com/pricing/)). Its only meaningful restriction is a ban on building no-code visual animation tools that compete with Webflow. A furniture configurator is nowhere near that ([GSAP licence](https://gsap.com/community/standard-license/)). The official `useGSAP` hook works with the Next.js App Router and cleans up all tweens and scroll triggers when a component unmounts ([GSAP React guide](https://gsap.com/resources/React/)). Pair it with **Lenis** for smooth scrolling. Lenis is MIT-licensed, a few KB in size, syncs with GSAP's ScrollTrigger, and switches itself off for users who prefer reduced motion ([Lenis](https://github.com/darkroomengineering/lenis)). Use **Motion** (MIT) for menus, drawers and cart micro-interactions. Loading it as `m` + `LazyMotion` costs **just over 5 KB plus 24 KB**, against 43 KB for the full component ([Motion docs](https://motion.dev/docs/react-reduce-bundle-size)). Motion+ ($399) is optional ([Motion+](https://motion.dev/plus)). Skip Barba.js (built for multi-page PJAX sites) and Theatre.js (its last release was in 2023) ([Theatre GitHub](https://github.com/theatre-js/theatre)). The View Transitions API now covers about **93%** of browsers ([caniuse](https://caniuse.com/view-transitions)), but Next.js still marks its `viewTransition` support as "not recommended for production" ([Next.js docs](https://nextjs.org/docs/app/api-reference/config/next-config-js/viewTransition)). Until that changes, use a GSAP curtain-overlay page transition through the small `next-transition-router` package ([next-transition-router](https://docsearch.algolia.com/mcp/docs/repo/ismamz/next-transition-router)).

Free Codrops tutorials cover the effects that award-winning sites use: SVG-mask image reveals ([Codrops, Mar 2026](https://tympanus.net/codrops/2026/03/11/svg-mask-transitions-on-scroll-with-gsap-and-scrolltrigger/)), an infinite parallax gallery with Flip transitions ([Codrops, Jul 2026](https://tympanus.net/codrops/2026/07/30/building-an-infinite-gsap-scroll-gallery-with-parallax-and-flip-transitions/)), and cinematic 3D scrolling ([Codrops, Nov 2025](https://tympanus.net/codrops/2025/11/19/how-to-build-cinematic-3d-scroll-experiences-with-gsap/)). GSAP's official `imageSequence` helper turns 36–72 compressed frames into a scroll-scrubbed 360° rotation of a hero chair, with no 3D needed ([GSAP helper](https://gsap.com/docs/v3/HelperFunctions/helpers/imageSequenceScrub)). A suggested homepage sequence is: a cream preloader that draws a gold arch outline; the headline revealed line by line over a deep-green sofa photo that opens through an **arch-shaped mask** echoing the "M" logo; a marquee of craft words; a pinned "from our Adyala Road workshop" story; the chair rotation; and a curtain transition into product pages. Free MIT component libraries (**Magic UI**, **Motion Primitives**, **Cult UI**, **shadcn/ui**) drop straight into Next.js. **React Bits** is free for commercial sites under MIT + Commons Clause, and **Aceternity**'s free tier permits client sites but not reselling the components ([Magic UI](https://magicui.design/components); [React Bits](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/DavidHDev/react-bits/main/README.md); [Aceternity licence](https://ui.aceternity.com/licence)). Avoid their neon, glow and meteor effects, which clash with green, gold and cream. Slow eases over 0.8–1.2 seconds read as premium. Never hide the hero image behind a preloader that waits for JavaScript, because that directly delays the page's main content appearing.

For type, the safest default is **Cormorant Garamond for headings with Manrope for the interface**. Both are SIL OFL, self-hostable through `next/font`, and free of any redistribution concerns. A more distinctive alternative is **Gambetta + Satoshi** from Fontshare. These are free for commercial use under ITF's Free Font License, but the files cannot be redistributed, and sources disagree on whether app embedding is covered, so check each font's licence page before using them ([licenseorg](https://licenseorg.com/guide/fonts/fontshare); [ITF](https://www.indiantypefoundry.com/news/introducing-fontshare)). For Urdu, use **Gulzar** or **Noto Nastaliq Urdu** (both OFL, on Google Fonts) ([Fontsource Gulzar](https://fontsource.org/fonts/gulzar/about); [Google Fonts](https://fonts.google.com/noto/specimen/Noto+Nastaliq+Urdu/about)), and load them only on Urdu pages. **Do not use Jameel Noori Nastaleeq.** Its only notice says it is "Free Of Charge For Urdu Lovers", which gives no clear commercial licence ([Ubuntu-pk list](https://lists.launchpad.net/ubuntu-pk/msg00007.html)). For icons, use **Phosphor** (MIT) in its light or thin weight, with **Lucide** (ISC) for utility icons ([Lucide licence](https://lucide.dev/license)).

Colour needs one firm rule. Contrast ratios calculated with the WCAG formula show that **gold text on cream fails (#C9A227 on #F5EFE0 = 2.11:1)**, while gold on bottle green (#0F3D2E) passes at **5.0–5.8:1** and ivory on green reaches **10.6:1**. On green, "gold" can be used for text. On cream, gold text must be a deep bronze such as #7A5E12 (5.33:1), and true gold is limited to thin lines, arch outlines and icons. Emerald, gold and cream is a well-established luxury pairing ([Muffin Group](https://muffingroup.com/blog/luxury-color-palette/)). By contrast, **no award-winning website using Mughal arches or jali was found**, so the motif is unclaimed territory. Use it sparingly: arch-shaped image masks, 1px gold rules, and a jali pattern at 4–8% opacity in the footer. No ready-made CC0 jali or arch SVG pack was found; free marketplace vectors carry their own licences or trial limits ([Vecteezy](https://www.vecteezy.com/vector-art/6058442-islamic-geometric-pattern)). Drawing three or four custom SVGs (the arch, an 8-point khatam star, a jali tile and a border) gives Mehal fully owned, tiny, animatable assets. For layout ideas, look at the free Framer templates **Forma House** and **NORDIQ** ([Forma House](https://www.framer.com/marketplace/templates/forma-house/); [NORDIQ](https://marketplace.framer.com/marketplace/templates/nordiq)), **Ekomia**, an Awwwards nominee with a Three.js configurator ([Awwwards](https://www.awwwards.com/sites/ekomia)), and **Woodwrights**, a craft maker that received an Awwwards Honorable Mention in October 2026 ([Awwwards](https://www.awwwards.com/sites/woodwrights)). On product pages, copy **Hem**'s material samples in a "Technical details" section ([Hem help](https://help.hem.com/en/articles/2728051-where-can-i-find-samples-and-swatches)) as a "Material Story" block: frame wood, upholstery, gold legs, foam density, and a short workshop video.

## One Supabase database and a phone-first admin beat a separate CMS

The owner needs to upload furniture photos, descriptions, materials and price ranges, and to receive design requests. **Supabase** keeps the catalogue and the incoming requests in a single Postgres database. Its free tier offers 500 MB of database, **1 GB of file storage**, 5 GB of egress and 50,000 monthly users. There are two catches: **projects pause after a week of inactivity**, and **image transformations require Pro at $25 a month** ([Supabase pricing](https://supabase.com/pricing)). Sanity's free plan is the most generous managed CMS (100 GB of assets and 100 GB of bandwidth with an image CDN) ([Sanity pricing](https://www.sanity.io/pricing)), but design requests would then live in a second system. **Payload 3** installs inside the Next.js app at `/admin` ([Payload](https://payloadcms.com/blog/payload-30-the-first-cms-that-installs-directly-into-any-nextjs-app)), and G2 reviewers rate it easier to use than Sanity ([G2](https://www.g2.com/compare/payload-cms-vs-sanity)). It is the right choice if a full CMS is wanted later. No research compared how well these admins work for uploading from a phone, so the safest choice for a non-technical owner on Android is a purpose-built **"Add product" page**. It would offer camera or gallery input, compress images in the browser to stay within the 1 GB limit, and include material chips (sheesham, deodar, kikar, lasani, velvet, foam), a min–max PKR price, a category and "configurable" flags.

Notifications should cost nothing at launch. When a customer finishes a design, the app saves it with a reference number and opens a `wa.me` link with a prefilled summary and a link to a read-only design page, and the owner also receives an email. Do not adopt the WhatsApp Cloud API yet. From **1 October 2026** Meta charges Pakistani businesses **$0.0473 per marketing message and $0.015 per utility or service message**, about **11 times India's rates**, and has removed free utility templates inside the 24-hour window. Each number still gets 1,000 free service messages a month ([ProPakistani](https://propakistani.pk/2026/09/15/meta-charges-pakistan-11x-more-than-india-for-whatsapp-business-messages/)).

Hosting has a licensing trap. **Vercel's Hobby plan is contractually limited to "personal or non-commercial use"** ([Vercel Terms](https://vercel.com/legal/terms)), so a shop belongs on **Vercel Pro at $20 per user per month** or on the Contabo VPS the team already runs, which costs nothing extra but needs someone to handle SSL, updates and backups. For images, Vercel Hobby includes 5,000 transformations a month, and going over a limit usually means waiting 30 days ([Vercel Hobby](https://vercel.com/docs/plans/hobby)). Cloudinary's free tier gives 25 monthly credits, where one credit is 1,000 transformations or 1 GB of storage or bandwidth ([Cloudinary pricing](https://cloudinary.com/pricing)), which is plenty for a catalogue of a few hundred pieces.

## Conclusion

The research changes where Mehal should spend its effort. Software is effectively free, and so are textures, fonts and animation. The scarce resources are **good modular 3D models of a few bestsellers** and **the workshop's discipline in limiting options to what it can build**. Mehal does not need 3D models for its whole catalogue. Four to six configurable hero pieces cover the products people actually customise, and AI redesign handles the long tail and uploaded inspiration photos at a few cents each. Both paths should end in the same place: a structured specification sent to WhatsApp and confirmed by a person. That fits a market that pays a 40–60% advance by hand and trusts a conversation more than a checkout.

The competitive opening is real but narrow in time. Interwood already has AR models and corner-sofa customisation, and adding on-screen configuration would be a short step for them. Mehal's lasting advantage is therefore not the 3D viewer itself. It is the pairing of a visual tool with a local, made-to-order promise: Adyala Road showroom visits, swatches photographed from real stock, carved sheesham for jahez buyers, and price ranges that local rivals do not publish. Several questions remain open and should be settled with small tests before committing. ARCore support on the Infinix and Tecno phones Mehal's customers carry was not checked. How faithfully the AI models copy a fabric swatch has not been measured. Fontshare's terms for app embedding are disputed. Test each on real devices and real photos before relying on it.
